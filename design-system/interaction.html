1 · The scroll-driven ops stepper

The product's centrepiece. A tall scroll region drives a pinned frame through seven steps — scrolling is the timeline; nothing autoplays.

The scroll track is height: calc(8 * 100vh) — eight viewport-tall buckets: a lead-in plus the seven steps. The viz panel is a child with position: sticky, so it stays pinned while the track scrolls past it.

    STEPS = ['design','validation','sourcing','manufacturing','quality','fulfillment','insights']
  1. Map scroll → step. p = -rect.top / (scrollHeight - innerHeight), clamped to [0, 0.9999]; idx = floor(p × 7).
  2. Publish state. The step name is written to data-active on the .ops element. Nothing else reads scroll position.
  3. CSS reveals. All seven screens are stacked position:absolute; inset:0 in one shared frame. .ops[data-active="x"] .ops-screen[data-screen="x"] fades its screen in; the rest stay at opacity:0.
  4. Jump & lock. Clicking a nav piece flips data-active instantly, smooth-scrolls to that bucket, and locks the scroll handler for the flight so in-between steps don't flicker.
Frame-fit invariant: every viz stage is height:100%; min-height:0 so it conforms to the one shared, viewport-bounded frame. Never give a stage a fixed min-height — it will exceed the frame and overflow:hidden clips the bottom. Reclaim height by keeping the title and margins tight, not by growing the frame.

Below 820px the whole mechanism collapses: the sticky scroll-jack is dropped and the screens stack in normal document flow. Any change to the stepper must be re-checked there.

3 · Viz entrance choreography

When a screen becomes active it doesn't just appear — its stage plays a short, staggered entrance, then idles. The pattern is consistent across all seven:

  1. Screen fade. opacity 0→1 over 500ms, translateY(8px)→0 over 600ms.
  2. Staged children. Inner elements reveal on incremental transition-delays (typically ~120–200ms apart) so the stage assembles rather than pops.
  3. Bespoke beat. Each viz then runs its own one-shot: the globe slow-spins, the QC grid scans cell-by-cell, the manufacturing bar fills, the insights chart draws. These start on activation and are cancelled when the step changes.
Entrances are one-shot and tied to data-active. Only the empty puzzle slot and the closing CTA glow loop forever — everything else settles and holds still. This restraint is the brand: motion is for arrival, not decoration.

4 · The film reveal (Act I)

Before the product, the cinematic open. A .stage.activated class (set after the cold-open beats) cross-fades the whole scene in on long, staggered delays:

  1. Bloom + spark fade/scale in over ~1500–1800ms; the vignette lifts to opacity:0 at the same time.
  2. Wordmark eases in at +1100–1200ms; the labyrinth mark follows, its three strokes drawing via stroke-dashoffset (staggered 1350/1650/1950ms), the centre dot at +3000ms.
  3. Nothing bounces. The scene breathes for 5–12s and never springs.

Reduced motion

Every pattern above must honour it. @media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) kills the stage/bloom/spark/typewriter transitions and ambient drifts. New motion must add itself to that block — the stepper still works (it's scroll state, not animation), it just stops being animated.
